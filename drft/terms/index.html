2. What DRFT does

DRFT is a wake-word listener for iPhone and Mac. You pick the words that matter — your name, a project code, a topic — and DRFT alerts you when those words come up in calls, meetings, or conversations happening around you. AI-generated summaries are a DRFT+ feature and are generated on your device by Apple Intelligence.

On iPhone, DRFT uses the device microphone to hear what is audible in the room — designed for speakerphone calls and in-person conversations, not a direct tap into the call line. On Mac, DRFT uses Core Audio process taps to capture the audio output of other applications on your system (Zoom, Teams, Meet, etc.) — no microphone is involved and no screen content is accessed. On both platforms, speech recognition runs on-device. Audio is processed in real time and discarded immediately — never recorded, stored, or transmitted.

3. Free and DRFT+ tiers

  • DRFT+ — Up to 10 wake words; up to 100 saved moments; up to 3 wake-word profiles; on-device AI summaries with Apple Intelligence; iCloud sync across your devices. Sold as a one-time purchase.

How wake words are counted. Wake words are one unified pool — there is no “default” versus “custom” distinction. Every word you keep counts toward your tier’s limit, and every word is editable and deletable. Onboarding seeds your first wake words from the name you enter, but those words carry no special status afterward. Keeping zero wake words is a valid state. Wake-word profiles are DRFT+ only; a new profile starts empty, and the words in the active profile are the ones DRFT listens for.

Saved-moment limits. When you reach your tier's cap, DRFT keeps the most recent moments and drops the oldest. Detection itself is never limited — only how many saved entries are retained.

AI summaries. Summaries require DRFT+ and an Apple Intelligence–capable device with Apple Intelligence enabled. Where a device cannot run them, DRFT says so rather than offering an alternative path; there is no cloud fallback, and no ad-supported or rewarded unlock.

5. Recording consent & ambient audio — your responsibility

DRFT does not record calls in the traditional sense — it processes audio in real time solely for wake-word detection, and no audio is saved at any point. However, because DRFT listens to ambient audio (iPhone) and to the audio output of conferencing apps (Mac), those streams may include conversation content, which means monitoring and consent laws may apply.

On Mac specifically, Core Audio process taps can capture the audio output of apps including video-conferencing tools. That audio may include the voices of remote participants on a call. DRFT processes this audio transiently on-device and does not record or store it, but you should be aware that the audio is being processed for wake-word detection, and that a short transcript window around each detection is saved.
